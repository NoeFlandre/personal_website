While reading through some write up today, I stumbled upon a familiar notion: "Attention Pooling". If I say familiar here, it's because I have already encountered this concept before and I even wrote a blog post about it a while ago: https://noeflandre.com/posts/2026/attention-pooling

But in fact, while reading these two words today, it didn't click right away. I could not remember exactly what it was about and how it was implemented in its nitty-gritty details. Combine this with the fact that I feel uneasy with  knowing something only vaguely, and I had to dig again on what was this "Attention Pooling" thing. 

So I took 10 minutes and went through my own blog post. And in the matter of a few minutes, I was back to speed on this concept.  And I suddenly realized how effective it has been for me to write about this concept before. 
Sure, I could have watched a 30 minutes video on it, or ask an LLM for a terse explanation. But somehow learning something is deeply personal. The learning process one may have could differ dramatically from yours. Some people need to look at math, some others prefer visual diagrams, another group need to hear the concept at loud and so on. 

I think writing blog posts has several great benefits, especially in the process of learning things, and I felt like today's experience with Attention Pooling was the right occasion to sing the praises of writing blog posts. 

## Reading before writing

The first thing that comes to mind, is that before writing anything, especially on a technical topic, you may almost certainly need to read some documents, watch some videos, or at least gather information through one way or another. This first step in the process of writing blog posts is in itself greatly valuable. 

Oftentimes you will need to combine several sources, maybe a video and a blog post, maybe a research paper and an LLM to break down your understanding. All this process of digesting information can be tough but it's super important to learn something. On top of that, you will most probably find a Youtube channel you enjoy along the way or a good writer you enjoyed a post from or even the next concept you want to write about! 

Serendipity is something I like to nurture, and reading before writing is a great mean to do so. 

## Writing

Once you have a decent understanding of the topic, it's time for you to write about it. I like to write my blog posts with a paper and a pen first, especially when it involves math. This step is about distilling down what you have learnt so far. You will most probably revisit some sources you went through, break down the math yourself and there are many things you won't understand at a first glance. So you are going to break down the dimensions you are working with, asking an LLM to challenge your understanding of it, draw some diagrams to visualize a tough concept ad what not. 

All this back and forth of making the concept crystal clear in your mind is exactly what is super valuable in your write up. What helped you in grasping something may help your audience just as much. For instance, I like to break down things a lot in my blog posts, even when it could seem dead simple, because somehow it helps me to understand the quantity I am manipulating, and it clears many doubts I came across . 

Despite the fact that writing is deeply satisfactory in itself, I think it is a great way to also master the language you choose to write in. For instance, as a non native speaker, I find it very useful to go through that exercise. I most often would google words I do not know yet, find new expressions to sound a bit more fluent and it involves some great skills too: active vocabulary, recalling things, maximizing clarity, summing up your chain of thoughts...etc 

## Collaborating

Once you are done writing your piece, you will maybe write it again on your computer, which is another learning layer on top. But the best part is to share your piece with some people you are close to. This is a good way to have your first reviewers. These could be some friends, some colleagues, anyone you think is willing to give it a look and provide you some feedback. 

This is an important step too, because it will give you a new glance on your write up, from someone who is coming with a fresh context, and usually  some parts you assumed were clear, are in fact blurry for your audience. People are usually happy to give you feedback, and they spot many things you never even noticed. That's also a great way to already share your work with some readers before hitting publish!

## Sharing

Once your piece is polished, here comes the time to share it with the world. What helped you understand a specific piece of knowledge, may now help anyone on Earth! 

And maybe many people will not like learning things through blog posts, but surely some will share some parts of your understanding through what you wrote, and you maybe saved your audience a great deal of struggles by digesting and distilling your understanding into words!
Your audience may come back to you with feedback, ask questions, challenge some parts of your writing, which is super helpful and rich to reinforce your own understanding and help them strengthen theirs. It also gives you a mean to share publicly the topics you are excited about and show that you are putting in the efforts to teach about them. 

Just like sharing your code on GitHub or opening your datasets on HuggingFace, writing blog posts is another mean to showcase publicly what you are working on. A CV can only speak so much, and it's pretty common nowadays to share a personal website while applying for some positions.

## Reviewing your own writing

Finally a last benefit of writing blog posts is that you can keep track of what you learned and most importantly revisit your own write ups. That's exactly what I have been doing for Attention Pooling and it helped me a lot to remember about something I once knew perfectly. In some sense it's like saving a checkpoint of yourself: "me_knowing_attention_pooling.pt".

If you poured the efforts to break down something clearly once, there is a great chance you grasp back all of it when revisiting it, since you now have a piece of knowledge tailored just for you!

Write blog posts!
